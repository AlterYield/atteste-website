### Current Art Behaviour

**Discovery channels:**
- Art advisor Marcus (primary for acquisitions)
- Gallery relationships (longstanding with 10+ galleries in London, NYC, Berlin)
- Museum acquisition committee (sees works before the public)
- Auction house previews (Christie's, Sotheby's, Phillips — VIP client)
- Frieze London, Art Basel, FIAC (regular attendee, VIP programme)
- FT, Apollo Magazine, Burlington Magazine
- Private collector dinners and studio visits

**Buying behaviour:**
- 5–8 acquisitions per year, £5,000–£80,000 range
- Research-intensive: exhibition history, institutional holdings, auction records, critical reception
- Works with advisor but makes final decisions herself
- Negotiates firmly — knows fair market value
- Buys at auction occasionally (secondary market for specific artists)
- Has deaccessioned (sold) 12 works over the years to refine the collection

**Current collection management:**
- Desktop folder system with subfolders by artist
- Paper files in a cabinet: invoices, certificates, condition reports
- Spreadsheet started 8 years ago, now unwieldy and incomplete
- Insurance schedule managed by broker (updated annually, always a hassle)
- Photographs of works taken by a professional every 3 years (expensive, time-consuming)
- Condition reports from conservators filed separately

**Pain points:**
- **Documentation overload:** 140 pieces across paper files, digital folders, and spreadsheets — nothing integrated
- **Insurance administration:** Annual revaluation is a weeks-long project involving broker, advisor, and conservator
- **Provenance verification:** Some older purchases lack complete paper trails
- **Deaccession records:** Sales records not systematically linked to original acquisitions
- **Location tracking:** Works rotate between two properties; no system for tracking which is where
- **Exhibition loans:** Occasionally lends to museums; tracking loan history is manual
- **Succession planning:** Has started thinking seriously about what happens to the collection. Her children have different interests — one loves art, one doesn't care
- **Advisor dependency:** If Marcus retired tomorrow, much institutional knowledge about her collection would vanish
