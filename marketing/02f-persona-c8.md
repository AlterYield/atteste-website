- **9:00 PM** — Gallery DM on Instagram. A gallerist offers him first access to a new body of work from an artist he collects. R15,000 per painting. He asks for installation views and a recent price comparison. Makes a decision in 20 minutes. Buys two
- **11:00 PM** — Updates his spreadsheet. Logs the purchase. Realises he forgot to update three other works from last quarter. Gives up. Goes to bed

### Current Art Behaviour

**Discovery channels:**
- **Twitter/X** — primary research platform. Follows auction houses, art market analysts, collector-investors, and a few artist accounts. Engages in price-discussion threads
- **Auction results** — Studies Strauss & Co, Aspire Art Auctions, and Bonhams Africa. Analyses hammer prices, lot withdrawal rates, and artist price trajectories
- **Art market reports** — Reads ArtTactic, Art Basel/UBS reports, and any data on SA/African art market performance
- **Instagram** — follows galleries and artists but primarily for visual research, not engagement
- **Collector WhatsApp groups** — two groups: one general SA collectors group and one specifically focused on art-as-investment
- **Gallery relationships** — building. Has 3-4 gallerists who offer him early access based on consistent buying patterns
- **Art fairs** — Attends Investec Cape Town Art Fair and FNB Art Joburg with a pre-researched buy list

**Buying behaviour:**
- 6–8 acquisitions per year
- Range: R5,000–R25,000 per work (stretches to R40,000 for high-conviction plays)
- Buying thesis: "Emerging SA artists with international gallery representation or trajectory toward it. Buy before the first major institutional show"
- Buys ~70% from galleries (primary market), ~20% from auctions (secondary market deals), ~10% directly from artists
- Holds most works but has flipped 3 works profitably in the last 18 months (average 2.3x return)
- Maintains a "watch list" of 15 artists — tracks every auction result, every exhibition, every institutional acquisition
- Thinks in terms of "entry points," "price discovery," and "exit liquidity"
- Buys editions/prints as lower-risk entries into artists' markets before committing to unique works
- Payment: EFT, credit card, occasionally uses his TFSA (tax-free savings) for art purchases (knows the tax implications)

**Current collection management:**
- **The Spreadsheet:** A Google Sheet with columns for artist, title, medium, size, date acquired, price paid, estimated current value, source, and notes. His most prized possession — and his biggest time sink
- Phone photos of each work (inconsistent quality)
- Email folder called "Art Receipts" (incomplete — some purchases confirmed via WhatsApp only)
- No certificates of authenticity for 6 works (bought directly or at fringe events)
- No insurance (has thought about it but hasn't acted)
- No condition reports
- Follows artist price indices informally (manual tracking from auction results)
- Has started a basic valuation model in Excel using comparable sales data

**Pain points:**
- **The spreadsheet is a nightmare:** Manual updates take hours. Data entry errors creep in. He once overestimated his portfolio value by R60,000 because of a formula error
- **No reliable valuation data:** SA art market data is fragmented. He spends hours cross-referencing auction results from different platforms
- **Provenance is weak:** For works bought via Instagram DM or WhatsApp, he has no formal documentation. This will hurt resale value
- **Missing certificates:** 6 works without COAs. He knows this devalues them but hasn't had time to chase artists/galleries
- **Can't benchmark properly:** No tool lets him track his art portfolio's performance the way he tracks his equity portfolio
- **Insurance procrastination:** Knows he needs it, keeps pushing it off because documentation is a mess
- **Framing backlog:** 5 works sitting unframed. Refilwe is losing patience
- **Resale friction:** When he flipped works, the lack of proper documentation slowed the process and arguably reduced the price
- **Credibility gap:** In collector circles, his analytical approach is respected but his documentation is amateur. He wants to be taken seriously as a collector, not just a speculator

### The Emotional Core

Thabo is not buying a collection manager. He is buying:

1. **Edge** — The informational advantage he's been building manually, now automated and reliable. Better data = better decisions = better returns
2. **Credibility** — The ability to present himself as a serious collector, not just a speculator with a spreadsheet. Documentation quality signals collection quality
3. **Efficiency** — Hours of manual spreadsheet work replaced by automation. Time is money — literally, in PE
4. **Confidence** — Decision-making backed by real data instead of gut feel and fragmented research. He can defend every purchase with evidence
5. **Evolution** — A quiet, unacknowledged transition from pure investment to genuine appreciation. The platform creates space for the collector he's becoming, not just the investor he presents himself as

This is a performance optimization sale. Attesté makes Thabo a more effective collector-investor — and, gradually, a deeper appreciator of art.
