### Current Corporate Collection Operations

**Inventory management:**
- Master Excel spreadsheet: 1,200+ rows with columns for artist, title, medium, dimensions, year, acquisition date, price, location (building + floor + room), condition status, last assessed date, insurance value
- The spreadsheet is the single most important document in Nomsa's professional life — and it crashes regularly due to size
- Secondary spreadsheets per building for floor managers who need to know what's on "their" walls
- Annual physical audit: Nomsa and Palesa spend 3 weeks walking every floor of every building, checking works against the spreadsheet. They find discrepancies every year: works moved without notification, works in storage that aren't recorded, works damaged without report
- No barcode, QR code, or digital tagging system

**Condition & conservation:**
- Condition reports: Word document template, completed per-work. Stored in a shared drive with inconsistent file naming
- Conservation assessments outsourced to a conservator annually for high-value works; Nomsa and Palesa handle routine assessments
- Environmental monitoring: non-existent in most buildings. Art is subject to air conditioning, direct sunlight, and cleaning chemicals without systematic protection
- Damage reports: ad hoc. Employees sometimes notify Nomsa; sometimes she discovers damage during audits months later

**Documentation:**
- Certificates of authenticity: exist for ~60% of works. Paper originals in a fireproof cabinet; inconsistent digital copies
- Provenance documentation: strong for recent acquisitions (last 10 years), weak for historical acquisitions (pre-2010 — before Nomsa's tenure)
- Artist information: maintained in individual artist folders, not linked to specific works in the inventory
- Photography: professional photos for ~40% of the collection (commissioned 5 years ago). Remaining 60% have phone photos or no photos at all

**Reporting & compliance:**
- Annual report to the art committee: collection overview, acquisitions, deaccessions, condition summary, budget utilization
- Insurance asset register: required annually in a specific format. Nomsa reformats her spreadsheet every year — 2 days of work
- B-BBEE reporting: the collection's role in transformation and enterprise development requires specific data extraction. Another manual process
- Financial reporting: artworks are fixed assets on the balance sheet. Finance requires depreciation schedules and valuation updates. Nomsa provides these, but the interface between her spreadsheet and the finance system is entirely manual

**Pain points:**
- **The spreadsheet will collapse:** 1,200 works, 14 buildings, embedded images, multiple users. Excel was never designed for this. One corruption event and years of data could be lost
- **Location tracking is fiction:** Works move — for renovations, executive preferences, events — and the spreadsheet isn't updated in real time. At any given moment, 5–10% of works are not where the system says they are
- **Audit is a marathon:** 3 weeks per year walking every floor of every building. This is unsustainable and will only get worse as the collection grows
- **Condition monitoring is reactive:** Damage is discovered after the fact because there's no systematic inspection schedule or employee reporting mechanism
- **Insurance is a liability:** If a major loss occurred today, the insurance claim would be complicated by outdated valuations, missing documentation, and location uncertainty
- **No executive visibility:** The art committee and C-suite have no real-time visibility into the collection. Every query requires Nomsa to manually extract and format data
- **Integration with corporate systems:** Finance, facilities, insurance, and compliance all need collection data in different formats. Nomsa is the human API between these systems
- **Professional isolation:** No peer network in SA, no established corporate collection management standards, limited professional development opportunities
- **Palesa dependency:** If Palesa leaves, 50% of operational knowledge goes with her. The system is people-dependent, not process-dependent

### Nomsa's Attesté Journey

**Month 0 — Business Case**
- Nomsa sees Attesté at a conference presentation on corporate collection management
- Immediately recognises the fit. Requests a demo. Brings Palesa
- Key question: "Can this replace my spreadsheet, generate insurance reports, track locations across 14 buildings, and produce board-level dashboards?"
- Answer: "Yes, and it can also do condition monitoring via QR codes, automated audit workflows, and integration with your finance reporting"
- Nomsa's challenge: she needs internal approval. Builds a business case:
  - Current cost of manual processes: ~R180K/year in her and Palesa's time on admin
  - Risk cost: an uninsured loss event could cost R500K+ due to documentation gaps
  - ROI: platform cost vs. time saved + risk mitigated = positive ROI within 6 months

### The Emotional Core

Nomsa is not buying software. She is buying:

1. **Professional recognition** — The ability to demonstrate that her role is a skilled, strategic function — not administrative overhead. The platform elevates her work from spreadsheet management to cultural curation
2. **Operational sanity** — Freedom from the constant anxiety that something is wrong, something is missing, something is damaged, and she doesn't know about it. The platform replaces fear with visibility
3. **Career trajectory** — With operational burdens automated, she can focus on the work that advances her career: curation, programming, strategic leadership. Attesté transforms her from collection manager to collection director
4. **Institutional resilience** — A system that doesn't depend on any individual person. If Nomsa moves on, the collection doesn't collapse. This is responsible stewardship — and it's what she'd advocate for even if it didn't benefit her personally
5. **The collection's dignity** — 1,200 works, R45M in value, 30 years of cultural investment — deserves better than an Excel spreadsheet. Nomsa has always known this. Now she has a tool worthy of the collection she's devoted her career to protecting

This is a professional transformation sale. Attesté doesn't just manage a collection — it redefines what corporate collection management can be.
