### Psychographic Profile

**Values:** Heritage, stewardship, family legacy, cultural patronage, South African identity
**Personality:** Deliberate, generous but private, storyteller, nostalgic yet forward-thinking, paternal
**Self-image:** "I didn't just collect art. I collected relationships, stories, and a piece of our country's cultural history"
**Art identity:** A patriarch of the SA collecting scene. Known personally by major galleries, artists, and auctioneers. Has lent to the Iziko South African National Gallery and Norval Foundation. His collection is recognized as culturally significant

### A Day in Richard's Life

- **6:30 AM** — Coffee on the stoep. Watches the mountain. The Maggie Laubser landscape above the fireplace catches the morning light — he remembers buying it in 1994, the year everything changed
- **8:00 AM** — Reads Business Day, Die Burger, and checks emails. Gallery invitation to a retrospective opening. Forwarded to Annette: "Shall we go?"
- **9:30 AM** — Call with his estate attorney. Discussing the trust structure. Attorney asks: "Have you got a current inventory of the art collection with valuations?" Richard pauses. "Not... exactly current"
- **11:00 AM** — Walks through the garden. Passes the William Kentridge bronze installed near the pool. A housekeeper asks who made it. He tells the story — 15 minutes, with tangents about meeting Kentridge in the '90s
- **1:00 PM** — Lunch with Annette and old friends. Conversation turns to a mutual friend whose family fought over artworks after he passed. Sobering
- **3:00 PM** — Liesel calls from London. "Dad, Stefan and I were talking — we should really document everything properly. What if something happens?" Richard agrees but doesn't know where to start
- **5:00 PM** — Walks through the house. Stops at each work. Each one is a memory, a friendship, a moment in South African history. "Someone needs to know these stories"
- **8:00 PM** — Dinner. Annette mentions she saw an article about a digital collection platform. "Maybe Liesel's right"

### Current Art Behaviour

**Discovery channels:**
- No longer actively "discovering" — curates and refines an established collection
- Gallery relationships built over decades (Goodman, Stevenson, Everard Read, SMAC)
- Auction catalogues from Strauss & Co, Aspire (SA auction houses)
- Referrals from artist friends and gallerists
- Occasional art fair (Investec Cape Town Art Fair — VIP)
- Has a semi-retired art advisor he's worked with for 20 years

**Buying behaviour:**
- 2–4 acquisitions per year, now very selective
- Range: R50,000–R500,000 per work
- Buys to fill gaps in the collection or acquire "important" works by artists he already collects
- Has started deaccessioning: sold 15 works in last 3 years to refine the collection
- Increasingly interested in commissioning site-specific works for the wine estate
- All purchases now made with legacy value in mind: "Will this matter in 50 years?"

**Current collection management:**
- Annette keeps a handwritten inventory book (started in the 1990s, last updated 2022)
- Insurance schedule managed by broker — last full revaluation was 2021 (overdue)
- Paper files in a study cabinet: invoices, some certificates, correspondence with artists
- Professional photos taken once, 12 years ago — many works acquired since then
- Condition reports exist for perhaps 30% of works
- Stories and provenance knowledge live entirely in Richard and Annette's memories
- No digital records whatsoever

**Pain points:**
- **Knowledge is mortal:** If Richard and Annette are gone, the stories behind 380 works vanish with them
- **Documentation is archaeological:** Finding a specific invoice means digging through 35 years of paper files
- **Succession is unresolved:** Three children, three properties, 380 works. Who gets what? How do they divide fairly? What about works with sentimental vs. financial value?
- **Insurance is dangerously outdated:** Collection has appreciated significantly since last valuation; current coverage is likely inadequate
- **Location chaos:** Works rotate between properties and some are in storage; no current system tracks locations
- **Deaccession records:** Sales not systematically linked back to original acquisitions or provenance chains
- **Artist relationships:** Some artists are now elderly themselves; the oral history of commissions and studio visits will be lost
- **Technology gap:** Richard is comfortable with email and WhatsApp but has never used a specialized app for anything personal
- **Annette dependency:** She is the operational memory of the collection. If she is unable to manage it, the system collapses

### Richard's Attesté Journey

**Week 1 — Activation (Family-Assisted)**
- Liesel discovers Attesté in London, sets up the account, and calls Richard: "Dad, I'm going to help you with this"
- Liesel creates the account on Premium tier — "You need everything, don't argue"
- First session: Richard and Annette sit together, uploading works room by room. Richard dictates; Liesel enters data remotely via shared access
- First 20 works catalogued with photos, dates, and basic provenance
- Richard is sceptical about the technology. Then the AI identifies a work from a photo and pulls up the artist's exhibition history. "How does it know that?"
- **Key moment:** "Annette, look — it has all of Maggie Laubser's exhibition history. We should have done this years ago"

**Month 1 — The Great Documentation**
- Family project: Liesel coordinates remotely. Stefan helps photograph works at the wine estate during a weekend visit. Pieter inputs financial data (the accountant in him takes over)
- 120 of 380 works catalogued
- Richard starts recording Collection Memoirs: voice notes and written stories for significant works
  - The Kentridge bronze: "We commissioned this in 2003. William came to the farm to see the site. He said the light reminded him of..."
  - The Pierneef: "This was the first serious work I bought. 1991. Annette said it was too expensive. She was right — and wrong"
  - The Sekoto watercolour: "I found this at a house sale in Pretoria. The family didn't know what they had..."
- Annette starts adding conservation notes she's kept in her head for decades
- **Key moment:** "I'm not just cataloguing art. I'm writing our family's cultural autobiography"

**Month 2–3 — Structure & Security**
- All 380 works catalogued. Provenance complete for 290 works; in-progress for remaining 90
- Insurance Vault export sent to broker. Broker's response: "You were underinsured by approximately R8 million. We need to adjust immediately"
- Legacy Vault activated:
  - Three-way split drafted: works allocated to children based on conversations about preference and sentimental value
  - "Untouchable" works designated: 15 pieces that Richard wants kept together as a family trust
  - Donation wishes: 8 works earmarked for eventual museum donation
  - Notes for each child: "Pieter — I know you don't love art the way your sisters do. But this Stern painting was in my office for 20 years. It watched me build the business. I'd like you to have it"
- Location tracking set up: every work tagged to a property
- **Key moment:** "For the first time in 35 years, I know exactly what we have, where it is, what it's worth, and who it goes to"

**Month 6 — Peace of Mind**
- Collection fully documented with stories, provenance, and succession plan
- Deaccession of 5 more works: complete chain of ownership maintained in Attesté
- Lends 3 works to Norval Foundation exhibition: loan documentation generated from platform
- Family WhatsApp now includes occasional Attesté links: "Look what your father wrote about this painting"
- Stefan has started his own small collection at the wine estate — uses Attesté from day one
- Richard and Annette feel genuine peace: "Whatever happens, our children won't have to guess. They'll know"
- **Key moment:** "Liesel told me: 'Dad, this is the most important thing you've done for us since the trust.' She's right"

### The Emotional Core

Richard is not buying software. He is buying:

1. **Permanence** — The assurance that 35 years of stories, relationships, and meaning won't evaporate
2. **Fairness** — A way to divide the collection with thought, not chaos
3. **Peace** — The comfort of knowing his children won't fight, won't guess, and won't lose
4. **Dignity** — The sense that his collection will be understood and respected after he's gone
5. **Connection** — Collection Memoirs become letters to his children, wrapped in art

This is not a technology sale. This is an emotional legacy sale that happens to use technology.
