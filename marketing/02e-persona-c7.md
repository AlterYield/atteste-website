### Psychographic Profile

**Values:** Pan-African identity, cultural fluency, authenticity, community, visibility for African artists globally
**Personality:** Charismatic, restless, opinionated, generous with her network, fiercely loyal to artists she believes in, impatient with pretension
**Self-image:** "I'm not a collector in the way old money thinks of collecting. I'm building a visual archive of who we are right now"
**Art identity:** A taste-shaper. Gallery directors ask her which emerging artists they should watch. Instagram curators repost her finds. She doesn't just buy art — she amplifies artists. Her support has material impact on early careers

### A Day in Zanele's Life

- **7:00 AM** — Alarm. Scrolls Instagram before getting out of bed. A Lagos-based artist she follows has posted new studio work. Screengrab, send to two friends: "Have you seen this? 🔥"
- **8:30 AM** — Coffee at a Maboneng spot. Laptop open. Finishing a branding deck for a Pan-African fashion brand. Background: Sho Madjozi playing
- **10:00 AM** — Client call — Accra. A cultural festival wants her to consult on visual identity. She knows six artists who'd be perfect for the commission. Pulls names from memory and WhatsApp threads
- **12:00 PM** — Lunch with a gallerist friend at The Cosmopolitan. He tells her about a young Congolese painter in Lubumbashi. "Send me images." She'll look tonight
- **2:00 PM** — Agency work. Creative review for a luxury brand campaign. She pushes back on stock African imagery: "This isn't what contemporary Africa looks like. Let me show you"
- **4:00 PM** — Receives a DM from a Nairobi photographer she bought from last year. "Zanele, my work is in a group show in Berlin!" She reposts, tags the gallery, adds commentary. 200+ likes in an hour
- **6:00 PM** — Gallery opening in Rosebank. Knows half the room. Introduced to a young sculptor from Limpopo. Takes his details, promises to visit his studio
- **9:00 PM** — Home. Finally looks at the Congolese painter's work. Extraordinary. Messages the gallerist: "I want the small blue one. How much?" Price is R18,000. Done within 10 minutes via EFT
- **11:00 PM** — Packing. Flies to Lagos on Thursday for a client meeting and the Lagos Art Week fringe programme. Texts her friend: "Is the Uthman Wahaab still at yours? I might bring someone to see it"

### Current Art Behaviour

**Discovery channels:**
- **Instagram** — primary discovery engine. Follows 400+ artists, curators, galleries, and art writers across Africa and the diaspora
- **WhatsApp groups** — part of at least 5 art-adjacent groups (SA collectors, Pan-African creatives, Lagos art scene, a women-in-art group, and a private group of early-career artist supporters)
- **Art fairs** — Investec Cape Town Art Fair, 1-54 (London/Marrakech), Lagos Art Week (fringe, not always the fair itself), Art X Lagos, FNB Art Joburg
- **Gallery relationships** — Blank Projects (CPT), Stevenson, WHATIFTHEWORLD, Afriart (Kampala), Rele Gallery (Lagos), Gallery 1957 (Accra)
- **Studio visits** — Goes directly to artists. Prefers buying from the artist when possible
- **Word of mouth** — Her network IS the discovery channel. She trusts people, not algorithms
- **Art media** — Contemporary And, Frieze (Africa coverage), ArtThrob, occasional Art Review

**Buying behaviour:**
- 6–10 acquisitions per year
- Range: R3,000–R35,000 per work (sweet spot: R8,000–R20,000)
- Buys impulsively when conviction hits — "If I love it and I can afford it, I buy it now. Tomorrow it's gone"
- Buys directly from artists (40%), galleries (35%), online/DM (15%), art fairs (10%)
- Increasingly trades and gifts works within her collector community
- Has never resold a work for profit and finds the idea uncomfortable
- Currency diversity: buys in ZAR, USD, NGN, GHS, GBP, EUR depending on location
- Payment methods: EFT, bank transfer, PayPal, sometimes cash at studio visits
- Documentation varies wildly: some invoices, some WhatsApp confirmations, some nothing at all

**Current collection management:**
- An Instagram saved folder called "My Collection" with photos of some works (not all)
- WhatsApp conversations as the only record of some transactions
- A Notes app list of "what I own" (incomplete, last updated 8 months ago)
- Photos scattered across three phones (current + two old ones) and Google Photos
- No provenance documentation for about 40% of works
- No insurance
- Works spread across three locations in two countries with no formal tracking
- Artist contact details in phone contacts, Instagram DMs, and WhatsApp — no central directory

**Pain points:**
- **Scattered collection:** Works in Joburg, Durban, and Lagos. She genuinely cannot say with 100% certainty what is where
- **Lost documentation:** A WhatsApp thread from 2021 has the only proof she owns a particular photograph. If she changes phones carelessly, it's gone
- **No provenance trail:** For works bought directly from artists in studios across Africa, there's often no paper trail. "I paid cash to an artist in Accra. I have the work. But can I prove I bought it? Not really"
- **Growing value, zero protection:** Several artists she bought early have exploded in value. The R5,000 painting might now be worth R80,000. She has no insurance and no formal record
- **Cross-border complexity:** Works in multiple countries with different legal frameworks. What happens if the Lagos friend moves? What happens to the work stored there?
- **Artist relationships at scale:** She's bought from 40+ artists. Keeping track of who, what, when, and for how much is impossible from memory alone
- **Sharing is clunky:** When she wants to show someone her collection (a curator, a writer, a friend), she scrolls through phone photos. "Wait, I know I have a picture of that one somewhere..."
- **No exhibition record:** Some of her works have been in exhibitions, publications, and catalogues. She doesn't track this and has no way to update provenance
- **Fear of loss:** Not insured. A fire at the Maboneng loft would destroy R800K+ of art with no documentation for a claim

### Zanele's Attesté Journey

**Day 1 — Discovery**
- Sees a fellow collector's Attesté collection showcase shared on Instagram Stories. It's beautiful — clean grid of works, artist names, acquisition dates. "Okay, what is this?"
- Taps through to Attesté. The landing page speaks to her: "Your collection, beautifully organized." Sees the free tier
- Signs up in 3 minutes. Immediately starts adding works
- First 10 works uploaded from phone photos within 45 minutes
- **Key moment:** AI identifies a Wycliffe Mundopa painting from the photo and pulls up exhibition history. "This thing is smart"

**Week 1 — The Obsession Phase**
- Zanele is competitive — even with herself. She wants her entire collection catalogued
- Adds works every evening. It becomes a ritual: glass of wine, music, upload session
- Hits the free tier limit at 25 works. Doesn't even hesitate: upgrades to Standard ($9/mo)
- Starts retroactively documenting purchases: goes through WhatsApp history, email, bank statements
- Messages 8 artists directly: "Hey, can you send me a certificate or invoice for the work I bought? I'm cataloguing everything properly"
- Several artists respond enthusiastically — they want their provenance chains clean too
- **Key moment:** "I've bought 65 works and I literally didn't have a proper record of 26 of them. That's insane"

**Month 1 — The Network Effect**
- Collection fully catalogued: 65 works, all photographed, all with acquisition details
- Shares her Attesté collection with three close collector friends: "You need to get on this"
- Two sign up within the week
- Uses Collection Showcase to share a curated selection with a curator who's researching Pan-African photography collections. Curator is impressed by the presentation and provenance detail
- Creates her first location tags: Maboneng (38 works), KwaMashu (15 works), Lagos (12 works)
- Finally knows exactly what's where
- **Key moment:** "A curator just asked if she can reference my collection in her essay. This is happening because I can actually show it properly now"

**Month 2–3 — Power User**
- Starts using Attesté as her buying workflow:
  1. Discover artist → add to watchlist
  2. Buy work → immediately log in Attesté with photo, invoice, receipt
  3. Track the artist's career from within the platform
- Connects with other collectors on the platform — discovers shared tastes and starts trading recommendations
- Gets insurance for the first time: exports the Insurance Vault document, gets a quote, insures the full collection for R1.8M
- Starts tagging works by theme for potential future exhibitions: "Women's Bodies," "Urban Africa," "Spiritual Landscapes"
- **Key moment:** "Attesté isn't just where I track my art. It's how I think about my collection"

**Month 6 — Cultural Capital**
- Her Attesté collection has become a reference point. Gallery owners check it. Curators reference it. Artist friends link to it in applications
- She's lent 4 works to a group exhibition — loan documentation generated seamlessly
- Has started writing short notes for each work (not as extensive as Richard's memoirs, but personal and sharp): "Bought this in Accra, 2024. Kofi was working out of a shipping container. Now he's showing at 1-54. Remember this feeling"
- Refers 11 people to Attesté. 7 sign up. She's an organic ambassador
- **Key moment:** "Someone called me 'one of the most important young collectors in Africa' in an article. I showed them my Attesté. That's what made them take my collection seriously"

### The Emotional Core

Zanele is not buying a catalogue tool. She is buying:

1. **Legitimacy** — The ability to present her collection with the same professionalism as institutional collectors, despite building it informally and across borders
2. **Proof** — Evidence that she was there first. That she supported these artists when no one was watching. Provenance as cultural credit
3. **Control** — Finally knowing what she has, where it is, and what it's worth — across cities, countries, and currencies
4. **Community** — Connection with other collectors who share her values, taste, and mission of supporting African contemporary art
5. **Amplification** — A tool that makes her advocacy for artists even more powerful. When she says "collect this artist," she can show her own collection as proof of conviction

This is a cultural identity sale. Attesté becomes the digital expression of who Zanele is as a collector and as a cultural participant.
