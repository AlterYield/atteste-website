New · Talking Provenance

The artwork now speaks.

For 500 years, art provenance has been paper. Receipts. Certificates. Gallery letters. Talking Provenance changes that. Record a Director’s Commentary on any work you’ve made — your voice, your studio, your context. The recording is hash-verified, attached permanently to the artwork, and travels with the piece to every future owner. The strongest provenance signal an artist has ever left on their own work.

This is First Witness. The very first commentary you record on a new work — at the moment of making — is its First Witness: the origin of its provenance chain, in your own voice, SHA-256-sealed and Bitcoin-anchored. Every later record links back to it, and because it’s recorded at creation it can never be backfilled. Provenance written forward, from the source.

Director’s Commentary

Sit with the work. Tap record. The Curator AI prompts gently for the details collectors care about — medium, year, intent, the moment of making. You stay in editorial control of every word that lands on the record.

Hash-verified Permanence

Each session is SHA-256 chained into the artwork’s provenance ledger. Tamper-evident, timestamped, and forensically grade. The same standing as a gallery invoice or a condition report.

3

Travels With the Work

When the piece sells, your commentary goes with it. The next owner — and every owner after — hears your voice describing what you made and why. A generational asset attached to your studio practice.

How Artists Use Attesté

A simple flow from catalogue to certificate to collector relationship.

1

Set Up Your Profile

Public artist page with bio, statement, links, and selected works. The page lives at attesté.art and is indexable on the gallery directory.

2

Catalogue Your Works

Upload images, dimensions, medium, year, edition, and your own narrative. Bulk import from CSV or add one piece at a time.

3

Issue Source Certificates

When you sell, generate a tamper-evident certificate of attestation. Each one carries a public QR-verified record that proves the piece came from you.

4

Stay in the Loop

When a collector logs an encounter or asks to verify a piece they bought from you, you get a notification. Approve, decline, or add provenance.
