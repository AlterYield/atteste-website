Certificate of authenticity for sculpture

A sculpture's certificate of authenticity should give the material and how it was made, the foundry or fabricator, the cast number within the edition, the patina or finish, height × width × depth with the weight, and whether the base is part of the work. How do I number cast editions?

Write the cast's number over the total, for example Cast 3/8. Artist's casts sit outside the numbered edition and are marked AC, AP or E.A. ("E.A. 1/4"). In the statement, give the total of everything cast from the mould, "Edition of 8 plus 4 artist's casts", so a buyer knows how many exist. If a cast was made after the model was finished, give both dates; any posthumous cast must say so.

What should I say about the foundry?

Name the foundry or fabricator and the city, the casting process (lost-wax, sand casting, fabricated steel, 3D-printed and cast), and where the foundry mark sits on the piece. Foundry records are often where later owners and researchers go to trace a cast, so the name matters.

Which other details matter for sculpture?

  • Material and finish. "Bronze, brown patina", "Carrara marble", "stoneware, wood-fired". For carved or modelled unique pieces, the edition is "Unique".
  • Size and weight. Height × width × depth, with and without the base, and the weight. Shippers and insurers need both.
  • Base or plinth. Whether it is part of the work, who made it, and how the work attaches.
  • Signature and marks. Where the signature, number and foundry mark are, often on the base or the back.
  • Care and siting. Indoor or outdoor, waxing schedule for patinas, mounting hardware, number of parts if it assembles.

How should I photograph a sculpture for its certificate?

Choose one primary view, the angle you would put in a catalogue, against a plain background with soft light. That single file is what the fingerprint covers. Keep other angles and close-ups of the marks with your own records.
