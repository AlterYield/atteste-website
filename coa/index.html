What should a certificate of authenticity include?

A COA is a written record, issued by the artist (or the artist's estate, publisher or gallery), that describes one specific work. Buyers, insurers and later owners look for:

  • Title, artist and year — the year the work was finished.
  • Medium and support — "oil on linen", not just "painting".
  • Dimensions — height × width (× depth), and whether they include the frame or base.
  • Edition — "Unique", or this impression's number out of the total, plus any proofs.
  • An image of the work, so the certificate cannot be mistaken for another piece.
  • A signed statement from the artist, and the date it was issued.

What does the SHA-256 fingerprint show?

SHA-256 turns a file into a 64-character code. The same file always gives the same code, and changing even one pixel, or re-saving the JPEG, gives a different one. Printing the code on the certificate ties it to the exact image file you chose.

That is all it shows: that an image file is unchanged since the certificate was issued. It says nothing about who made the work or whether the statement is true. The certificate is your own statement of record, and Attesté does not examine the work or check what you write.

What does the QR code do?

The QR code opens a page on atteste.art that shows the record printed on the certificate: title, artist, year, medium, fingerprint and issue date. The record travels inside the link itself, so nothing is stored on a server. If a long title makes the code too dense to scan at 30 mm, the code leaves out dimensions and edition first; the PDF always prints every field.

Who should issue a COA?

The artist, or someone with the standing to speak for the work: the artist's estate, the publisher of an edition, or a gallery acting for the artist. A COA written by a seller with no link to the artist adds little. Sign every certificate by hand and keep a copy.
