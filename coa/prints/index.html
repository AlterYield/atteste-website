Certificate of authenticity for prints and editions

A certificate of authenticity for a print should state the technique, this impression's number within the edition, and the full count of proofs outside it: artist's proofs (AP), printer's proofs (PP), the bon à tirer (BAT). How does edition numbering work?

An edition number like 12/50 means this is impression number 12 of an edition of 50. The number identifies the impression; it does not have to match the order the sheets came off the press. Write the number exactly as it appears in pencil on the print, usually in the lower-left margin, with the signature at lower right.

What are AP, PP, BAT, TP and HC?

  • AP — artist's proof (also E.A., épreuve d'artiste). Impressions kept by the artist, outside the numbered edition. Conventionally around 10% of the edition. Number them too: "AP 2/5".
  • PP — printer's proof. Impressions given to the printer or workshop.
  • BAT — bon à tirer. The approved proof the edition was matched against. Usually one.
  • TP — trial proof. Working proofs pulled before the edition was fixed, often different from it.
  • HC — hors commerce. Impressions marked "not for sale", traditionally for exhibition or archive.

Put the full count in the statement, for example "Edition of 50, plus 5 AP, 2 PP and 1 BAT". A buyer can then see every impression that exists, not only the numbered ones.

What else belongs on a print's certificate?

  • Technique. Etching, aquatint, drypoint, lithograph, screenprint, woodcut, linocut, or giclée/pigment print for digital editions. Name every technique used.
  • Paper. Maker, name and weight, e.g. Somerset Satin 300 gsm.
  • Plate or image size and sheet size.
  • Printer, workshop and publisher, and any chop mark (blind stamp) on the sheet.
  • The matrix. Whether the plate was cancelled or destroyed, the screens cleared, or the file retired after the edition.

One certificate per impression?

Yes. Each impression gets its own certificate carrying its own number, "12/50" on one and "13/50" on the next. Proofs get theirs too. The image and fingerprint can be the same across the edition.
